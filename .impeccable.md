## Design Context

### Users
This product is for internal use by a technically fluent operator monitoring Solana addresses and token activity in real time. The primary job to be done is to detect important address behavior and trading signals quickly, with minimal friction, so the operator can assess opportunities and react fast. The interface should optimize for scanning, fast comprehension, and confidence under time pressure rather than onboarding, storytelling, or broad-market approachability.

### Brand Personality
The product should feel professional, calm, and fast. It should evoke trust and a sense of high-end operational control rather than hype, noise, or novelty. The tone should be credible and restrained, with enough sharpness to feel live and responsive, but never chaotic or flashy.

### Aesthetic Direction
The visual direction should be a dark-first, premium monitoring console with blue-cyan accents. Light mode only needs to remain usable and coherent; the primary design effort should go into the dark theme. The interface should not look like a generic admin dashboard, commodity SaaS template, or loud speculative Web3 product. Favor focused information density, crisp hierarchy, controlled highlights, and subtle atmospheric depth over colorful card grids or decorative clutter.

References should lean toward high-signal trading tools, security consoles, and data products with disciplined hierarchy and polished contrast. Avoid purple-heavy gradients, playful SaaS illustrations, or overdone neon cyberpunk styling.

### Design Principles
1. Optimize for signal detection first. Important price, address, and strategy information should be scannable within seconds, with strong visual hierarchy and restrained secondary detail.
2. Make dark mode feel intentional and premium. Use layered dark surfaces, precise borders, and blue-cyan emphasis to create depth without noise.
3. Build trust through restraint. Prefer calm composition, consistent spacing, and stable contrast over visual gimmicks or trend-driven styling.
4. Keep the interface fast in tone and behavior. Interactions should feel responsive and operational, with minimal friction and no unnecessary flourish.
5. Avoid generic back-office patterns. The UI should feel like a purpose-built market monitoring instrument, not a standard CRUD admin panel.
6. Preserve accessibility through stable contrast. Ensure text, charts, controls, and status indicators remain legible with reliable contrast in the primary dark theme.
